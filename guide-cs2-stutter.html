CS2 stutters and microstutters: what causes them and how to find yours

Why CS2 stutters at a high frame rate: the graphics card, the engine, drivers or background programs. How to measure stutters and 1% low and what Windows can fix.

Average FPS in CS2 can be 700 and more and the game still stutters. The average is not the problem: single long frames are. At 240 Hz the screen refreshes every 4.2 ms. As long as frames fit into that time, motion is smooth. A frame that took longer than one or two refreshes is a stutter you can see.

Four sources of stutters

  • The graphics card is at 100%. Frames queue up and the frame time starts to jump. A sign: GPU load near 100% and stutters in heavy scenes (smoke, explosions, many players).
  • The game itself. Asset streaming, shader compilation after a driver update, engine pauses. Windows tuning cannot fix this.
  • A game thread did not get a core. The thread is ready to run, but the core is busy with another program or a driver. These waits are measured in microseconds, and the long ones cause stutters. More in the guide on DPCs and interrupts.
  • Background programs. A browser, launchers and overlays that wake up during the match and take a core.

The causes differ and so do the fixes. So: measure first, change things after.

How to find your cause

  • Toolbox, the Check page: one button, 30 seconds, a report on the desktop: FPS, 1% and 0.1% low, the longest frame, stutters, the game threads' waits and a hint about what limits the frame rate. Toolbox is free and runs on any Windows 10/11.
  • DomainSchedTest: several runs in a row and one table with means and ranges. The guide is on the Measure page.

Look at three numbers: 1% low and 0.1% low, the longest frame and the game threads' waits. If the waits are short but you still see stutters, the cause is the graphics card or the game, not Windows.

What Domain OS does, and what it does not

The Domain OS playbook removes what wakes up during a game and changes nothing without a description. Every change can be undone. Toolbox watches the game during the match: it gives the game's busiest threads cores that drivers and background programs do not use, while using almost no CPU itself.

On the developer's PC (Ryzen 7 7800X3D, RTX 5060, CS2 deathmatch, 3 runs each), going from Toolbox 1.16.1 to 1.17.0, the 1% low went from 429 M13 to 454 M15 and the 0.1% low from 230 to 294. That is one PC, so every measurement and the method are open on the Results page.

What Domain OS will not do: speed up a graphics card that already runs at 100%, or fix the engine's own pauses. If a measurement shows exactly that, we will say so.
